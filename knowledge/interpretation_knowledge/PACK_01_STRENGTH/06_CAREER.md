# 1. How to use this file

Career knowledge from Strength answers: **what kind of load and room fits this fuel system?**

It does not name a single destined job.

It does not say “quit tomorrow and become X” from Strength alone.

Suitable / unsuitable below are **environment families**, not banned professions. A Weak Day Master can be a surgeon in the right hospital. A Very Strong Day Master can fail as a founder in the wrong decade.

---

# 2. Very Strong

**Suitable load.** High-stakes operations, turnaround, command in crisis, building something against resistance, roles where someone must decide while others freeze.

**Unsuitable load.** Endless consensus theatre with no authority. Work that only rewards waiting, softening, and never closing. A job whose KPI is “make everyone comfortable” will feel like a cage, and you will become the problem in that cage.

**Management.** You can run a team that needs a spine. You must install deputies who tell you the truth, or you will manage a silent room.

**Employment.** You tolerate hierarchy if the hierarchy is going somewhere. A slow bureaucracy with no decision rights will turn you into a rebel or a bully.

**Entrepreneurship.** Possible when the market needs force. Dangerous when the company needs listening more than pushing. Co-founders who can brake you are not optional decoration.

**Leadership.** Lead from the front in short, sharp campaigns. For long culture-building, add people whose job is the slow layer. You will not do that layer for fun.

So what: pick arenas where surplus is the product, and buy a brake as part of the org chart.

---

# 3. Strong

**Suitable load.** Ownership of a hard piece: operations, delivery, professional practice with long apprenticeship, management of a real P&L, any role that rewards finishing.

**Unsuitable load.** Purely decorative titles. Roles that punish persistence and reward only novelty-without-follow-through. Also: unpaid family hero as a substitute for a career.

**Management.** You are believable. People believe you will still be there on Friday. Develop the skill of changing the plan without feeling that you betrayed the plan.

**Employment.** You are the backbone employee. Negotiate recovery time as a work condition, not a personal weakness. Otherwise the company will use the backbone until it cracks.

**Entrepreneurship.** Fits if the business is a long build. Less fit if the business is only hype cycles. You can outwork a trend. You cannot outwork a wrong product forever.

**Leadership.** Lead by carrying. Make sure the team is not only watching you carry. Teach the method, or you become irreplaceable in the worst sense.

So what: your career risk is **the noble trap** — a hard job that is merely hard, not right.

---

# 4. Balanced

**Suitable load.** Cross-functional work, product, policy, consulting, partnership roles, anything that must translate between tribes. Rotation does not scare you.

**Unsuitable load.** A role that demands a single mask 12 hours a day with no integrator function — pure enforcer, or pure servant, with no permission to move.

**Management.** You can hold a mixed team. Your risk is not setting a direction because you can see why every direction is incomplete. Set a direction anyway, then revise on a clock.

**Employment.** You do well beside a clearer extremist (a Very Strong principal, a specialist Weak expert) if your job is to keep the system human and coherent.

**Entrepreneurship.** Fits partnership better than lone-wolf myth. You need a closer or a specialist so the company does not live in eternal maybe.

**Leadership.** Lead as the person who can hear more than one tempo. Then still choose.

So what: your career asset is range. Your career failure mode is a beautiful unmade choice.

---

# 5. Weak

**Suitable load.** High-skill rooms with real support: research, craft, advisory, quality, client relationships, specialist medicine/law/design **with a functioning team**, teaching in a held setting.

**Unsuitable load.** Isolated high-volume grind, hostile sales floors, “figure it out alone” founder romance, night-after-night emergency culture with no recovery.

**Management.** You can manage if the organization gives you cover from above and a sane headcount. You manage by climate and craft, not by fear. Do not accept a manager title that is only a dumping ground for other people’s surplus.

**Employment.** Excellent when the employer is a source of structure. Disastrous when the employer is a black hole. Job interviews should test the room, not only your CV.

**Entrepreneurship.** Safer as expert practice with a partner who holds operations, or inside a platform. Solo chaos-startup is usually the wrong machine, not a bravery test.

**Leadership.** Lead a small expert group. Do not volunteer to be the company’s only battery.

So what: career success is **a feeding environment plus a defined craft**, not a louder personality.

---

# 6. Very Weak

**Suitable load.** Protected specialist work, writing/thinking roles with control of calendar, high-trust boutique practice, technical depth behind a stronger commercial front.

**Unsuitable load.** Open-plan hustle, political large-org warfare, any job whose unofficial requirement is performing inexhaustibility.

**Management.** Usually a poor default. If it happens, keep the span of control small and the rules explicit. Managing as emotional unpaid labor will empty you.

**Employment.** The employer must be a frame: clear hours, few fake urgencies, permission to be a specialist. Prestige without frame is a trap.

**Entrepreneurship.** Only in a tightly designed niche with administrative armor (people, systems, a front person). “Just hustle” is contraindicated.

**Leadership.** Counsel, not command. Influence through the quality of the held thought.

So what: the career question is not “what title?” It is **“what structure keeps this intelligence online?”**

---

# 7. Composition rule

Name environment families.

Do not print a list of 20 job titles as destiny.

Do not use Strength to forbid an industry that Useful God or Pattern has not spoken to.

If the customer already has a career, interpret **fit of load**, not a fantasy second life.
