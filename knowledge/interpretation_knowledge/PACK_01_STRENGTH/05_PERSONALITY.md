# 1. How to use this file

Personality here means **default operating style of the standing**.

It is not a full psychological diagnosis.

It is not a star-sign portrait.

Other packs (Ten Gods, Pattern) will add other layers. This file only says how **force or lack of force** shows in manner.

---

# 2. Very Strong

**Behavior.** Direct. Fast. Occupies the center without applying for it. Stillness is rare unless it is the stillness of someone who already won the point.

**Communication.** Short sentences. High volume of certainty. You summarize while others are still laying out context. People who need a preamble feel flattened.

**Thinking.** First principle: “Can this be forced through?” Strategy appears after the push, or not at all. You think in outcomes, not in atmospheres.

**Working style.** Sprint, dominate the blocker, next. Delegation happens late, because doing it yourself is quicker in the moment and more expensive over the year.

**Motivation.** Resistance. A closed door is an invitation. Ease can bore you. You may create a mountain so there is something to climb.

**Conflict style.** Front-foot. You would rather have the argument now. Withdrawal in others looks like games. You press. The other person either submits or leaves.

So what: this personality wins short wars and loses slow rooms unless a brake is installed on purpose.

---

# 3. Strong

**Behavior.** Reliable presence. You show up. You finish. You do not need the room to be inspired first.

**Communication.** Clear, a little dense. You explain by adding effort: more detail, more proof, more “I already tried that”. Humor may be dry. Soft disclaimers are not native.

**Thinking.** Linear persistence. You keep the original frame and work it. Reframing feels like quitting. Sometimes it is intelligence.

**Working style.** Long shift. Deep stack. You prefer ownership of a whole piece. Shared authorship is possible but not your first love.

**Motivation.** Competence and load. You want to be the person who can be trusted with the difficult object. Status matters less than not dropping it.

**Conflict style.** Stand your ground. You can stay in disagreement without panic. You may stay too long. Peace looks like the other side finally agreeing.

So what: people experience you as a pillar. Pillars do not move when someone needs the floor plan changed.

---

# 4. Balanced

**Behavior.** Situational. You match the room more than you stamp it. New acquaintances may not know your “real” setting for a while.

**Communication.** Inclusive. You name both sides. You ask a question where Very Strong would issue a line. In the wrong week this sounds like hedging. In the right week it sounds like adulthood.

**Thinking.** Comparative. You hold two models at once. This is excellent for design and diplomacy. It is slow for a burning kitchen.

**Working style.** Integrator. You move between people and pieces. You dislike being trapped in one function if it erases the others. Context-switching is native; deep-only caves can feel tight.

**Motivation.** Coherence. You want the system to make sense. Chaos that others find exciting feels like a tear in the fabric.

**Conflict style.** Mediation first. You try to keep the relationship and the truth. If that fails, you go quiet rather than scorched-earth. The quiet can be mistaken for agreement.

So what: you are easy to include and hard to pin. Pinning yourself, on purpose, is part of maturity for this standing.

---

# 5. Weak

**Behavior.** Responsive. You enter after you have read the weather. In a safe room you are warm and precise. In a harsh room you shrink or over-perform.

**Communication.** Attuned. You hear the sentence under the sentence. You may not put your own sentence on the table until asked twice. When you finally speak, it is often the thing the room needed.

**Thinking.** Contextual. You think in relationships between people, timing, and mood. Abstract dominance games bore or scare you. You are good at “what will this do to the system?”

**Working style.** Collaborative craft. You do excellent work beside a steady other. Isolated high-pressure ownership drains the style until it looks like incompetence. It is not.

**Motivation.** Belonging and usefulness. You want to contribute without being the storm. Recognition matters because the tank does not self-validate as easily.

**Conflict style.** Yield, then residue. You avoid the blow-up. The argument happens later, in your body, or in a sudden distance the other person did not see coming.

So what: your personality is an instrument. The room is the amplifier. Choose rooms.

---

# 6. Very Weak

**Behavior.** Conserving. You ration presence. Social performance is expensive. You may be vivid in a trusted two-person setting and almost absent in a loud group.

**Communication.** Sparse or carefully prepared. Surprise debate is not your arena. Written thought, delayed response, and one-to-one speech are kinder to this standing.

**Thinking.** Deep and easily interrupted. You need a held space to finish a thought. Open-plan urgency shatters the line.

**Working style.** Niche excellence or nothing. Wide, noisy, multi-stakeholder chaos is not a “growth challenge”. It is the wrong machine.

**Motivation.** Safety to exist. Not luxury — the basic condition that your energy will not be confiscated. From that condition, devotion to a craft can be extreme.

**Conflict style.** Exit or freeze. Fight is rarely worth the recovery. People may call you avoidant. Often you are staying alive.

So what: do not coach this personality into a Very Strong costume. Coach the **frame** that lets this personality be intelligent.
