# 1. How to use this file

Advantages are **usable capacities**, not compliments.

Each line must help a paying customer recognize a real asset they can put to work.

Do not rewrite Meanings with nicer adjectives.

Do not invent talents the class does not support.

If a cause that creates the advantage is absent in the case, do not use that advantage unit.

---

# 2. Facets

This file speaks through seven facets:

1. Decision making
2. Leadership
3. Learning
4. Discipline
5. Adaptability
6. Responsibility
7. Stress tolerance

A class may be strong in one facet and only adequate in another. That difference is the value.

---

# 3. Very Strong

**Decision making.** You can close a decision while others are still warming up. In a crisis, speed is an asset. Use it where delay is the real risk: operations, emergencies, stalled teams.

**Leadership.** People borrow your certainty. A frightened group will follow the person who does not look empty. That is a real gift in turnaround work.

**Learning.** You can drill. Repetition does not bore you as fast as it bores a depleted person. Skills that need hours of contact — craft, sport, instrument, a hard professional exam — fit this tank.

**Discipline.** You can hold a line you chose. Once the standard is yours, you will not quietly drop it because the room is tired.

**Adaptability.** This is not your headline gift. You *can* change method, but only when you decide the old method is finished. Do not sell Very Strong as flexibility. Sell it as **capacity to impose a new method once chosen**.

**Responsibility.** You will pick up the bag that everyone else walked around. Organizations survive on this. Families sometimes survive on this.

**Stress tolerance.** Acute stress is fuel. You can stay in the fire for a long day. That is the advantage. (The cost of never leaving the fire belongs in Challenges, not here.)

---

# 4. Strong

**Decision making.** You decide with enough inner weight that the decision stays decided. Teams trust you because you do not reverse from mood.

**Leadership.** You can be the person who still has battery at hour ten. That is how projects finish. Leadership here is **staying power**, not theatre.

**Learning.** You learn by doing the hard version. Theory is fine; you believe it after contact. Apprenticeship, on-the-job mastery, and roles that reward persistence fit.

**Discipline.** You can keep a practice without a cheerleader. Morning systems, long builds, and unglamorous maintenance are native.

**Adaptability.** You adapt by adding effort first. When that is the right move — a deadline, a physical task, a short sprint — you win.

**Responsibility.** You are employable in the deep sense: people can hand you a load and sleep. That is commercial value, not a personality trophy.

**Stress tolerance.** Chronic moderate pressure does not dissolve you. You can live in a demanding role without needing the room to be gentle every day.

---

# 5. Balanced

**Decision making.** You can see more than one viable path. In messy human systems — product, policy, family business — that prevents stupid binary choices.

**Leadership.** You can lead without turning the team into a copy of yourself. People with different paces can stay. That is rare, and it is worth money in mixed teams.

**Learning.** You can learn from both the forceful teacher and the careful one. You are less trapped in a single school of method.

**Discipline.** Your discipline is situational: you can tighten when the work needs tightness and loosen when the work needs air. That is a professional advantage in creative-operational hybrids.

**Adaptability.** This is the headline. New country, new boss, new brief — you can re-register without a identity crash. Careers with rotation, consulting, and cross-functional work fit.

**Responsibility.** You take enough responsibility to be trusted, without needing to own the entire building. That makes you a good deputy, partner, and integrator.

**Stress tolerance.** You do not only have one stress gear. You can absorb a spike and also notice when the spike should end. That is healthier than glory-under-fire, and it is an advantage in long careers.

---

# 6. Weak

**Decision making.** You decide better when the field is well framed. Give this person a clear brief, constraints, and a trusted counterpart, and the quality of judgment is often high — because they do not waste fuel on dominance theatre.

**Leadership.** You lead by making the room livable. People tell you the truth. In knowledge work, that is a leadership advantage Strong profiles often lack.

**Learning.** You learn from people. Mentorship, feedback-rich environments, and collaborative craft accelerate you. This is not dependence. It is **how your tank fills**.

**Discipline.** Your best discipline is selective. You can be meticulous in a chosen craft because you cannot afford scatter. Depth in one lane is the asset.

**Adaptability.** You read atmosphere early. You notice the deal going sour, the team mood shifting, the client’s real objection. That sensitivity is professional radar.

**Responsibility.** You take care of what is in your hands. You rarely smash a process just to prove authorship. Operations, care, research, and client trust grow from this.

**Stress tolerance.** You are not built for endless fire. Your advantage is **early warning**. You feel overload before the Strong person admits it. Organizations that listen to you avoid accidents.

---

# 7. Very Weak

**Decision making.** You decide well inside a protected frame: specialist judgment, one domain, enough time. Do not sell this class as a battlefield general. Sell **precision in a held space**.

**Leadership.** Quiet authority in a small, high-trust circle. People who need a safe thinker will come. That is a real niche: counsel, specialist, editor of other people’s force.

**Learning.** One-to-one teaching, slow mastery, and environments that do not humiliate depletion. When those exist, learning can be deep because nothing is wasted on performance.

**Discipline.** The useful discipline is conservation: saying no, keeping the calendar thin, repeating a small sustainable practice. That discipline is an advantage. It is how this life stays possible.

**Adaptability.** You adapt by changing the environment, not by inflating yourself. Finding the niche is the skill. That is a commercial advantage if the person is allowed to use it.

**Responsibility.** You are careful with what you accept. When you accept, you tend not to treat other people’s work as a toy. Trust in delicate work — quality, ethics, care — can be high.

**Stress tolerance.** Acute heroics are not the gift. The gift is knowing the true budget. A business that plans around a real energy budget outperforms a business that pretends everyone is Very Strong.

---

# 8. Composition rule

When composing Customer Mode Advantages:

- Pick three to five facets that match the class **and** the person’s actual life context if known.
- Do not print all seven for every customer.
- Do not praise a Weak Day Master for “unbreakable stress tolerance”.
- Do not praise a Very Strong Day Master for “effortless flexibility” unless other packs support it.
