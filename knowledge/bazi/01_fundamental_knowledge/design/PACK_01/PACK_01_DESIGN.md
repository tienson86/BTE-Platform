# 1. Purpose

## 1.1 Overview

Pack 01 – Fundamental Knowledge defines the foundational concepts required by the entire BTE Knowledge Canon.

This pack establishes the common conceptual vocabulary, academic definitions, and canonical relationships that every higher-level knowledge module depends upon.

No domain-specific interpretation, rule evaluation, scoring, prediction, or implementation logic SHALL be introduced in this pack.

The purpose of this pack is to provide a single, authoritative, and reusable foundation for all subsequent knowledge packs.

---

## 1.2 Objectives

The objectives of Pack 01 are:

- Define the fundamental concepts of BaZi.
- Establish canonical academic definitions.
- Eliminate duplicated definitions across domains.
- Provide reusable Knowledge Records.
- Support deterministic compilation into the Knowledge Canon.
- Enable explainable reasoning across engines.
- Serve as the root dependency of all higher-level knowledge packs.

---

## 1.3 Responsibilities

This pack SHALL:

- define foundational concepts;
- define canonical terminology;
- define academic relationships;
- define conceptual boundaries;
- provide authoritative references;
- support knowledge reuse;
- support compiler validation.

This pack SHALL NOT:

- calculate charts;
- evaluate strength;
- determine useful gods;
- perform fortune analysis;
- execute rule matching;
- contain scoring algorithms;
- contain inference logic;
- contain AI prompts.

---

## 1.4 Position in the Knowledge Canon

Within the BTE Knowledge Canon hierarchy, Pack 01 occupies the lowest academic layer.

All subsequent packs SHALL reference concepts defined in this pack rather than redefining them.

Knowledge dependency SHALL always flow upward from this pack.

```
Knowledge Canon
│
├── Pack 01  ← Foundation Layer
│
├── Pack 02
│
├── Pack 03
│
└── ...
```

---

## 1.5 Design Goals

The design goals of Pack 01 are:

### Academic Consistency

Every concept SHALL have exactly one canonical definition.

---

### Reusability

Knowledge Records SHALL be reusable across all BTE domains.

---

### Traceability

Every concept SHALL be traceable to its academic sources.

---

### Explainability

Every concept SHALL support transparent explanation by downstream engines.

---

### Machine Readability

Every Knowledge Record SHALL be fully compatible with the Knowledge Compiler.

---

### Long-Term Stability

Conceptual definitions SHOULD remain stable across future versions unless academic evidence requires revision.

---

## 1.6 Expected Outcomes

Upon completion of Pack 01:

- all foundational concepts are formally defined;
- all concept relationships are established;
- all Knowledge Records pass validation;
- all records compile successfully into JSON Canon;
- all records are eligible for inclusion in the Knowledge Graph;

# 3. Knowledge Architecture

## 3.1 Architecture Overview

Pack 01 adopts a layered knowledge architecture designed to maximize academic consistency, modularity, reusability, and machine readability.

The architecture separates conceptual knowledge from implementation logic, ensuring that every Knowledge Record remains domain-independent and reusable across the entire BTE platform.

Knowledge within this pack SHALL be organized as a directed dependency graph with clearly defined ownership and relationships.

---

## 3.2 Architectural Principles

The architecture of Pack 01 SHALL follow these principles.

### Single Source of Truth

Each academic concept SHALL be defined exactly once.

No duplicate definitions are permitted.

---

### Atomic Knowledge

Each Knowledge Record SHALL represent one primary concept.

A Knowledge Record SHALL NOT combine multiple independent concepts.

---

### Layered Dependency

Knowledge SHALL depend only on concepts defined at the same or lower architectural level.

Circular dependencies are prohibited.

---

### Explainability

Every relationship between concepts SHALL be explicitly represented.

Implicit academic assumptions SHOULD be avoided whenever possible.

---

### Machine Readability

Every Knowledge Record SHALL be fully compatible with the Knowledge Compiler and JSON Schema.

---

### Academic Independence

Academic definitions SHALL NOT contain implementation details.

---

## 3.3 Architecture Layers

Pack 01 is divided into four logical layers.

### Layer 1 — Core Cosmology

Defines the most fundamental principles.

Examples:

- Yin Yang
- Qi

Characteristics:

- no upstream dependency;
- highest academic priority;
- reusable by every downstream record.

---

### Layer 2 — Fundamental Models

Defines the primary theoretical framework.

Examples:

- Wu Xing

Depends on:

- Yin Yang
- Qi

---

### Layer 3 — Fundamental Entities

Defines canonical entities.

Examples:

- Heavenly Stems
- Earthly Branches

Depends on:

- Yin Yang
- Qi
- Wu Xing

---

### Layer 4 — Foundational Relationships

Defines relationships between entities.

Examples:

- Stem–Element correspondence
- Branch–Element correspondence
- Yin–Yang polarity
- Seasonal association

Depends on every previous layer.

---

## 3.4 Knowledge Hierarchy

The conceptual hierarchy SHALL follow the structure below.

# 6. Academic Source & Reference Strategy

## 6.1 Overview

This section defines the academic source policy governing all Knowledge Records contained within Pack 01.

The purpose of this policy is to ensure that every Knowledge Record is supported by authoritative references, follows consistent citation practices, and maintains long-term academic integrity.

Every academic statement included in this pack SHALL be traceable to one or more recognized sources.

---

## 6.2 Objectives

The objectives of this strategy are:

- establish authoritative academic references;
- standardize citation practices;
- ensure traceability of knowledge;
- resolve conflicting interpretations consistently;
- preserve historical authenticity;
- support future academic review.

---

## 6.3 Source Hierarchy

Academic references SHALL follow the hierarchy below.

### Level 1 — Classical Canonical Sources

These are the highest-priority references.

Examples include:

- 《渊海子平》 (Yuan Hai Zi Ping)
- 《三命通会》 (San Ming Tong Hui)
- 《滴天髓》 (Di Tian Sui)
- 《滴天髓阐微》 (Di Tian Sui Chan Wei)
- 《子平真诠》 (Zi Ping Zhen Quan)
- 《穷通宝鉴》 (Qiong Tong Bao Jian)

Statements supported by these sources SHALL be regarded as canonical unless superseded by explicit governance decisions.

---

### Level 2 — Classical Commentaries

These include authoritative annotations and explanatory works based upon classical texts.

Examples include:

- traditional commentaries;
- annotated editions;
- recognized historical interpretations.

These sources MAY clarify canonical meanings but SHALL NOT override Level 1 sources.

---

### Level 3 — Modern Academic Publications

Examples include:

- scholarly books;
- peer-reviewed papers;
- university publications;
- recognized academic research.

These sources MAY provide clarification or historical context.

They SHALL NOT redefine canonical concepts.

---

### Level 4 — Internal BTE Standards

Examples include:

- Knowledge Canon Standards;
- approved terminology;
- governance decisions;
- compiler specifications.

These sources define implementation standards but SHALL NOT alter academic doctrine.

---

## 6.4 Citation Requirements

Every Knowledge Record SHALL include:

- primary academic source;
- supporting references where applicable;
- citation notes when interpretation differs;
- source confidence assessment.

Knowledge Records without traceable references SHALL NOT be approved.

---

## 6.5 Citation Format

Each reference SHOULD include:

- original title;
- translated title (if applicable);
- author;
- dynasty or publication period;
- edition (if known);
- chapter or section;
- relevant passage reference.

Example

Title:

《滴天髓》

Author:

京图（traditionally attributed）

Reference:

Chapter XX

Notes:

Canonical definition of Yin and Yang transformation.

---

## 6.6 Source Traceability

Every significant academic statement SHALL be traceable.

Traceability SHALL include:

Statement

↓

Knowledge Record

↓

Academic Reference

↓

Source Location

↓

Citation Notes

The compiler SHOULD preserve reference metadata during JSON generation.

---

## 6.7 Handling Multiple Interpretations

Different schools of thought MAY present differing interpretations.

When multiple interpretations exist:

1. Canonical interpretation SHALL be identified.
2. Alternative interpretations MAY be documented.
3. Conflicting opinions SHALL be clearly labeled.
4. The rationale for selecting the canonical interpretation SHALL be recorded.

The Knowledge Canon SHALL avoid combining incompatible interpretations into a single definition.

---

## 6.8 Academic Neutrality

Knowledge Records SHALL present concepts objectively.

Authors SHALL NOT:

- introduce personal opinions;
- prioritize modern preferences without justification;
- reinterpret classical doctrine without evidence.

Where uncertainty exists, it SHALL be explicitly documented.

---

## 6.9 Translation Policy

The canonical language of classical references remains the original Chinese text.

Supporting translations MAY be provided in:

- English;
- Vietnamese.

Translations SHALL preserve the academic meaning of the original text.

When translation ambiguity exists, the original wording SHALL take precedence.

---

## 6.10 Reference Metadata

Each reference SHOULD include the following metadata.

| Field | Required |
|--------|----------|
| Original Title | Yes |
| English Title | Optional |
| Vietnamese Title | Optional |
| Author | Yes |
| Dynasty / Period | Yes |
| Edition | Optional |
| Chapter | Optional |
| Section | Optional |
| Confidence Level | Yes |

---

## 6.11 Source Quality Assessment

Every reference SHALL receive a confidence level.

| Level | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| High | Canonical classical source |
| Medium | Recognized commentary or academic publication |
| Low | Supporting reference requiring additional verification |

Only High and Medium confidence references SHOULD be used to establish canonical definitions.

---

## 6.12 Reference Governance

Academic references SHALL be reviewed periodically.

Reference updates MAY occur when:

- new critical editions become available;
- errors are identified;
- additional authoritative evidence is discovered.

Changes to canonical references SHALL require academic approval.

---

## 6.13 Prohibited Sources

The following SHALL NOT be used as canonical references.

- anonymous internet articles;
- unverified blogs;
- social media posts;
- AI-generated content without academic verification;
- commercial promotional materials;

---

## 8.2 Workflow Objectives

- ensure academic correctness;
- verify structural completeness;
- guarantee compiler compatibility;
- maintain governance consistency;
- preserve traceability.

---

## 8.3 Review Lifecycle

Every Knowledge Record SHALL follow the lifecycle below.

Draft

↓

Author Self-Review

↓

Academic Review

↓

Technical Review

↓

Reference Verification

↓

Compiler Validation

↓

Governance Review

↓

Approval

↓

Publication

↓

Freeze

No stage may be skipped.

---

## 8.4 Stage Definitions

### Stage 1 — Draft

The author prepares the Knowledge Record using the official template.

Requirements:

- mandatory sections completed;
- initial references identified;
- metadata populated.

---

### Stage 2 — Author Self-Review

The author verifies:

- template compliance;
- writing quality;
- relationship consistency;
- completeness checklist.

A record failing self-review SHALL NOT proceed.

---

### Stage 3 — Academic Review

Conducted by academic reviewers.

Focus:

- conceptual correctness;
- source quality;
- terminology;
- definition boundaries;
- consistency with the Knowledge Canon.

Possible outcomes:

- Approved;
- Minor Revision;
- Major Revision;
- Rejected.

---

### Stage 4 — Technical Review

Conducted by Knowledge Engineering reviewers.

Checks include:

- template integrity;
- metadata;
- identifiers;
- relationship references;
- schema compatibility.

---

### Stage 5 — Reference Verification

Verifies:

- Source IDs;
- bibliography consistency;
- citation completeness;
- reference hierarchy compliance.

---

### Stage 6 — Compiler Validation

Automated validation performed by the Knowledge Compiler.

Checks include:

- JSON generation;
- schema validation;
- dependency resolution;
- relationship validation;
- graph integrity.

Compilation errors SHALL block publication.

---

### Stage 7 — Governance Review

The Knowledge Canon Committee confirms:

- compliance with standards;
- impact on existing records;
- versioning policy;
- publication readiness.

---

### Stage 8 — Approval

Approval assigns:

- official version;
- publication status;
- approval metadata;
- effective date.

Only approved records may be published.

---

### Stage 9 — Publication

The record becomes part of the official Knowledge Canon.

Publication SHALL include:

- Knowledge Record;
- JSON artifact;
- graph update;
- documentation update.

---

### Stage 10 — Freeze

Frozen records become immutable except through the formal change process defined in Governance.
