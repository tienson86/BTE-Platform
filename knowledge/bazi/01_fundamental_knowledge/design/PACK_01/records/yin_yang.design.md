# 10. Canonical Definition

## 10.1 Short Definition

Yin Yang is the fundamental principle of complementary duality that describes the dynamic balance, interaction, transformation, and cyclical evolution of all Qi and phenomena within the BaZi cosmological framework.

---

## 10.2 Canonical Definition

Within the BTE Knowledge Canon, Yin Yang is defined as the most fundamental cosmological principle governing the manifestation, differentiation, interaction, and transformation of Qi.

Yin and Yang are not independent substances or opposing entities. They are complementary attributes that always exist in relation to one another. Every observable phenomenon possesses both Yin and Yang aspects in varying proportions.

The continuous interaction between Yin and Yang produces change, maintains balance, and drives the cyclical evolution of natural processes. This dynamic relationship forms the theoretical foundation upon which Wu Xing, Heavenly Stems, Earthly Branches, Seasonal Qi, and all BaZi analytical models are established.

Within the scope of this Knowledge Record, Yin Yang serves as a foundational theoretical model rather than an interpretive or predictive mechanism.

---

# 11. Core Meaning

The essential meaning of Yin Yang within the BaZi Knowledge Canon is to describe:

- complementary duality
- relative opposition
- dynamic balance
- mutual dependence
- continuous transformation
- cyclical change

These principles describe how Qi evolves rather than assigning auspicious or inauspicious qualities.

---

# 12. Fundamental Characteristics

## C01 — Complementary Duality

Yin and Yang always exist as complementary aspects of a unified whole.

Neither can exist independently.

---

## C02 — Relative Opposition

The distinction between Yin and Yang is relative rather than absolute.

A phenomenon may be Yin in one context and Yang in another.

---

## C03 — Mutual Dependence

The existence of Yin requires Yang.

The existence of Yang requires Yin.

Removing either destroys the balance of the whole.

---

## C04 — Dynamic Balance

Yin and Yang continuously seek equilibrium.

Balance is dynamic rather than static.

---

## C05 — Mutual Transformation

Under appropriate conditions Yin transforms into Yang and Yang transforms into Yin.

Transformation is continuous and cyclical.

---

## C06 — Mutual Consumption

An increase of Yin generally reduces Yang.

An increase of Yang generally reduces Yin.

This interaction maintains overall balance.

---

## C07 — Mutual Support

Yin nourishes Yang.

Yang activates Yin.

Both contribute to the maintenance of functional harmony.

---

## C08 — Infinite Divisibility

Every Yin contains Yin and Yang.

Every Yang contains Yin and Yang.

The principle applies recursively across all levels of observation.

---

## C09 — Universality

The Yin Yang principle applies universally throughout the BaZi theoretical framework.

It is independent of individual charts.

---

## C10 — Non-Predictive Nature

Yin Yang itself does not produce interpretation, scoring, fortune prediction, or judgment.

Interpretation arises only after integration with other BaZi components such as Wu Xing, Heavenly Stems, Earthly Branches, Ten Gods, Seasonal Qi, and Luck Cycles.

---

# 13. Constraints

This Knowledge Record SHALL NOT:

- determine chart strength
- determine favorable elements
- evaluate Ten Gods
- evaluate Patterns
- determine Luck quality
- calculate scores
- produce interpretation sentences

These responsibilities belong to downstream modules.
